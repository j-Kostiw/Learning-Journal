🔄 E-Learning Recap
Risk mitigation = reducing impact (can’t always eliminate).

Common strategies: firewalls, access control, backups, monitoring.

Policy types: Acceptable Use, Access Control, Incident Response, Monitoring.

Downtime impact: lost revenue, trust, productivity.

🛠️ Security Architecture & the Role of a Security Architect
Architects build secure systems and networks.

Develop cross-organisation policies, procedures, and response plans.

Require broad IT and InfoSec knowledge (IAM, operations, etc.).

🚨 Incident Response: What, Why, and How
❗What is an incident?
Any event that can lead to serious consequences for systems or data.
Types include:

Malicious code: viruses, worms, trojans

Unauthorised access: password misuse, privilege escalation

Policy violations: misuse of company resources

Espionage: data theft or surveillance

Hoaxes and scams

Aggressive probes: network monitoring with malicious intent

🔁 Phases of Incident Response
Minimise Impact

Stop the spread or escalation.

Decide whether to involve executives or go public.

Recover Efficiently

Fail over systems if needed.

Log all actions (prosecution-ready mindset).

Restore operations fast.

Secure Systems

Lock down vulnerabilities.

Audit and implement new controls.

Follow-Up

Confirm threat is removed.

Store evidence securely.

Share lessons learned across the team.

🧠 Mitigation vs Remediation
Mitigation: Contain the threat, reduce impact (e.g., isolate infected system).

Remediation: Eliminate the root cause (e.g., patch vulnerability, terminate rogue account).

👁️ Recognising Incidents
Detection methods:

Automated: IDS, antivirus, firewalls, logs

Human: User reports, admin observations, alerts

Signs: New user accounts, unusual traffic/times, deleted files

📜 Security Policies: The Backbone of Incident Response
Types:

Acceptable Use: What employees can and cannot do

Monitoring Policies: What traffic is tracked, employee awareness

Contact Lists: Who to call and when (critical for escalation)

User Responsibilities: Reporting, installations, behaviour

Admin Responsibilities: Logging, updates, education

Non-repudiation: Trust in origin/authenticity of data or actions (proof it happened).

📣 Education & Enforcement
Policy = worthless if:

Not accessible

Not explained

Not enforced

Recommendations:

Online/hardcopy versions available.

Mandatory reading/sign-off during onboarding.

Clear, non-vague language + glossary.

Refresher every 6 months (aligned with password updates).

Senior management must support and model policy behaviour.

🔎 Realistic Scenario: Corporate Website Incident
Summary:

VP spots manipulated public webpage during party.

Incident reported to Helpdesk → Security Manager → SysAdmin.

Server restored from backup; source tracked to disgruntled employee’s VPN account.

Access hole due to misconfiguration; closed after forensic review.

Employee terminated following HR and legal process.

Takeaway: Strong policy, clear POC lists, and efficient response = minimal damage and restored trust.
