1. Challenges of Traditional Project Management (Waterfall)
Data science and data engineering projects are dynamic, making rigid Waterfall methods ineffective.

Waterfall struggles with:

Rapidly changing requirements.

Long development cycles.

Lack of stakeholder feedback.

Complexity in handling evolving data.

2. Agile as a Solution
Agile methodologies (Scrum, Kanban) are better suited for data projects.

Key Agile principles:

Iterative development – frequent releases.

Close collaboration – continuous feedback.

Responsiveness to change – flexibility to adapt.

3. Agile Frameworks & Scrum
Scrum introduces key roles:

Scrum Master – facilitates team processes.

Product Owner – defines priorities.

Development Team – delivers product increments.

Scrum ceremonies:

Sprint Planning – defining work for the sprint.

Daily Stand-ups – short team updates.

Sprint Reviews – presenting work to stakeholders.

Retrospectives – continuous improvement discussions.

4. Lean & Six Sigma for Data Projects
Lean Principles: Eliminate waste, optimize workflows.

Six Sigma (DMAIC Framework):

Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control – used for data-driven improvements.

Agile and Lean principles can be combined to enhance efficiency.

5. Minimum Viable Product (MVP)
MVP Strategy: Deliver essential features first, gather feedback, and iterate.

Focuses on reducing time-to-market and maximizing value.

6. Case Study: Credit Bank Corporation
Developing an HR analytics dashboard.

Challenges included integrating new data sources.

Agile methodology was used to ensure adaptability.

7. Writing & Managing User Stories
User Stories should be:

Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable (INVEST).

Sprint Backlog & Scrum Board: Help manage task progress.

Burndown Charts: Track work remaining.

8. Integration of Agile, Lean, and Six Sigma
Encourages data-driven decision-making.

Helps in stakeholder collaboration and engagement.

Supports a culture of continuous improvement.

9. Hands-On Activities
Sprint planning for a real-world project.

Backlog refinement, prioritization, and estimation.

Addressing data protection concerns (GDPR).

10. Final Key Learning Summary
Agile outperforms Waterfall in dynamic data projects.

Scrum provides structure while remaining flexible.

Lean and Six Sigma enhance efficiency.

Combining these methodologies helps champion a data-driven culture.
